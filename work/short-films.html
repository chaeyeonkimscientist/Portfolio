Short Films

Sonic Storytelling Through 24-Hour Constraints

Chaeyeon Kim · 2024 – 2026

Composition · Sound Design · ML Audio

“What music reveals that dialogue cannot.”

TL;DR

Four short films, each built to prove the same thesis: music and sound design are narrative language. Across all four, the score is the film’s psychology, with instruments assigned to characters, ML models standing in for impossible casting, and structural motifs (Morse rhythm, de Broglie wavelengths) carrying meaning dialogue never states.

Two of the four have been recognized at the Minervopolis Film Festival.

Golden Owl Award Before You Learned to Forget Minervopolis Film Festival 2026 · top film out of 10+ Best Audio Award FLUSH! The Flowers Minervopolis Film Festival 2025 · out of 10+

Watch the films

The Philosophy

These films share one operating principle: composition, performance, and technical innovation are a single practice. Four commitments follow from that:

  1. 01

    Composition informs narrative

    Leitmotifs are character psychology.

  2. 02

    Technical constraints drive creativity

    ML models become the solution to problems that are otherwise impossible inside a 24-hour window.

  3. 03

    Sound design is storytelling

    The mix carries what dialogue withholds.

  4. 04

    Performance is research

    Embodying a psychological state is how you come to understand it well enough to score it.

This is the Hyperinstrument philosophy applied past the boundary of the traditional instrument: TTS models, sound-design systems, and biometric tools treated as instruments that “understand” and amplify a human psychological state.

01 · Before You Learned to Forget

2026 · Golden Owl Award — Minervopolis Film Festival 2026

Composer · Writer · Director

A departure from the psychological-horror triptychs that came before it — a healing, warming film in the register of a Pixar short. A woman frozen in time while the world rushes past rediscovers her childlike wonder through a series of “missions” delivered by a mysterious voice on a payphone. The voice turns out to be her own past self, embodied as an animated caterpillar that only she can hear — a being that, in its own eyes, sees butterfly wings already growing from her back.

The conceptual spine: physics as emotional language

The narration threads real physics through the emotional arc:

  • de Broglie wavelengths — every object with mass has its own lambda; the character’s grief is that her wavelength won’t harmonize with the crowd’s.
  • Frequencies beyond perception — “there are frequencies we can’t hear… until suddenly, one day, we can.” This is the film’s structural hinge: the payphone rings and only she hears it.
  • Resonance — “when two frequencies match, when wavelengths align” — used as the moment she remembers who she was.
  • Conservation of energy — Einstein’s “energy only changes form” reframed as: wonder is never lost, only misplaced.

The music

  1. 01

    Meter & tempo

    ¾ for two bars, then 4/4 at 100bpm. It is a loop of that until the instrumental/dance break scene. Represents the character’s feeling out of place inside a world moving in fast-forward.

  2. 02

    Tonality

    Locked in D major. Diatonic, bright, and almost stubbornly stable (Krumhansl-Schmuckler profiling returns D major at r = 0.839; the tonic triad F#–A–D accounts for nearly half of all weighted pitch content). The near-total absence of chromatic pitches means the film sounds innocent.

  3. 03

    Harmony that floats instead of resolves

    The tonic almost never appears as a bare triad, it’s dressed as Dmaj7, Dmaj9, Dadd9. Strong dominant-to-tonic (V–I) motion is deliberately avoided, so the music hovers on tonic prolongation rather than pushing toward resolution. This is the harmonic signature of the whole piece: coloristic, ambient, pastoral, closer to game/film “wonder” scoring than to classical cadence.

  4. 04

    The signature move

    A recurring C#augmented / C#sus4 — voicing the leading tone as a shimmering, unresolved sonority that pulls toward D without ever committing.

  5. 05

    The orchestral bed

    Cellos anchor the bass (down to G1), violas and violins fill the middle, and flute + glockenspiel + harp occupy the top octaves purely for shimmer. Heavy harp arpeggiation/glissando and glockenspiel sparkle at the very top (up to B6) are hallmarks of the pastoral-fantasy idiom. Tremolo strings (“Romance Strings,” “Violas trem”) are used as a breathing, swelling sustain device — velocities range from a whisper-quiet 13 up to 127, so the score genuinely breathes rather than sitting at a flat mezzo-forte.

The animation-as-past-self device

The past self is an animated caterpillar rendered directly onto live footage: crawling the bridge railing, pixelated on the payphone’s LED panel, riding on the character’s shoulder, then a cocoon, then a butterfly. In the caterpillar’s POV shots, watercolor butterfly wings shimmer on the grown woman’s back — wings she cannot see yet. The reveal that the caterpillar is the younger self is the film’s quiet plot twist, and the score marks it with the resonance motif.

02 · FLUSH! The Flowers

2025, Buenos Aires · Best Audio Award — Minervopolis Film Festival 2025

Writer · Producer · Composer · Actor

A character-driven psychological drama built on an instrument-as-identity system — the film’s central sonic idea, and the reason it took the festival’s audio prize. Each character is an instrument family, and their music behaves the way they do:

  • Allison (fragile) — woodwinds / flute: sharp, delicate, exposed. The line is thin and easily broken, sitting high where it feels vulnerable.
  • Brett (strategic) — strings: deliberate, controlled, calculating. Measured bowing and held tension; every phrase sounds decided.

The drama plays out not just in what the characters say but in how their instruments interact — when strings crowd out the flute, when the flute is left alone in silence. The mix is the subtext.

03 · STOP !

2025, Taipei

Co-Director · Writer · Composer · Actor

A 24-hour film built around a set of absurd mandatory constraints, each turned into a structural or sonic device rather than a gimmick:

Constraint Solution
Made in under 24 hours Full write–score–shoot–cut cycle in one day
Must feature Gordon Ramsay Built Ramsay with a machine-learning TTS voice model
Must feature a fortune cookie “Dirt” made from crushed cookies, message hidden inside
Must say “I wish I were on Jupiter” Delivered in the crosswalk sequence — Jupiter = the subconscious

The music

  • Morse code as rhythmic motif. The title-roll beat literally spells S-T-O-P in Morse — the sound design encodes the title before a word is spoken. The rhythmic grammar of the whole film grows out of dots and dashes.
  • Leitmotifs for cognitive augmentation. Recurring musical figures represent the film’s theme of the mind being extended/augmented — the score tracks a state of consciousness, not just a scene.

04 · How Have You Been Feeling Lately?

2024, Buenos Aires

Writer · Actor · Sound Engineer · Composer

The origin point of the psychological-triptych approach: three parallel therapy narratives, each a study in how childhood trauma resurfaces as adult behavior. Sound, space, and performance operate as one integrated system.

  • Sarah — perfectionism → emotional eating → filling parental voids.
  • Liz — abandonment → weaponized desirability → attraction worn as armor. (Delivers the mandatory line “Eat the Rich.”)
  • M — self-dissolution → becoming machinery → humanity traded away for utility.

The 24-hour constraints here also demanded a non-human character — resolved with a final figure who “wants” nothing, and whom you genuinely cannot classify as human or not — and a public-transport setting, resolved with a metro station that becomes the shared space where the three narratives echo.

The triptych

Foreground one panel at a time. The others drop to a murmur — three psychologies, one mix.

What These Projects Demonstrate

  • Music as expressive technology

    Hyperinstrument thinking pushed beyond traditional instruments. ML voice models, biometric-adjacent sound systems, and instrument-as-identity frameworks treated as instruments that understand and amplify psychological states.

  • Rapid prototyping under extreme constraint

    Custom tools (a Gordon Ramsay TTS model, character-mapped soundtrack systems, Morse-encoded rhythmic motifs) designed and shipped inside 24-hour and 3-day windows.

  • Sound design as storytelling

    Across four films, the score carries the load dialogue can’t: a chord that refuses to resolve, a fragile flute pressed by strategic strings, a beat that spells the title, three motifs competing for the foreground.
